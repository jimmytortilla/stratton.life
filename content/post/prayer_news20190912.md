<p>Late last month Azusa, the kids, and I traveled a few hours by highway from Kumamoto to just about the southernmost tip of Kyushu.  We spent four days and three nights in Ibusuki (pronounced <em>ee-boo-ski</em>)&ndash;famous for geothermal sand-baths&ndash;for the purpose of fellowship with ABWE missionaries and a few others as well.  What a treat it was for us to enjoy some time with missionaries! This time, however, none of the Stratton family chose to be buried alive in the hot sand.</p>
<figure>
    <img src="/images/ibusuki-kyushu.jpg"/> <figcaption>
            <h4>Ibusuki in Southern Kyushu</h4>
        </figcaption>
</figure>

<p>For me (Nate), perhaps the key-moment in our trip came as we were checking out of the hotel:  While standing in the lobby one of the missionaries, a sister in Christ gifted in encouragement, came up to me with a kind word combined with a gentle reminder.  In all gentleness and humility she told me that I have wonderful kids (YEP!) and&hellip; to lighten up.  It was a word aptly spoken.</p>
<p>Colossians 3:21 says, &ldquo;Fathers, do not provoke your children, lest they become discouraged.&rdquo; (ESV)  I believe that is what my sister was getting at.  When we (I) have high expectations of our (my) children&ndash;this is especially true of dads&ndash;we (I) tend to point out their faults and nitpick.</p>
<p>Later, on the way home, I came accross another reminder about fatherhood, this time in a public restroom of all places!  It was a small sign on the wall above the &hellip; you know &hellip; written in Japanese, English, Chinese, and Korean:</p>
<figure>
    <img src="/images/positive_message.jpg"/> <figcaption>
            <h4>A Message in the Men&#39;s Room</h4>
        </figcaption>
</figure>

<p>Besides making me chuckle, the sign made me think.  The English translation is pretty goofy; but the idea is good.  Instead of saying something like, &ldquo;Keep the bathroom clean!  It&rsquo;s really disgusting for those who have to clean up after you,&rdquo; it basically says, &ldquo;Good job!  Way to keep the bathroom clean!&rdquo;  Perhaps more importantly it says, &ldquo;Thank you.&rdquo;</p>
<p>How often do I appeal to my children&rsquo;s desire to please me?  And yet I know that they <em>do</em> want to please me.  It is so much nicer to be told that you <em>are</em> something good than to be told to <em>be</em> good.  Of course that does not mean that I plan to throw correction out the window; but it does mean that I want to try to avoid &lsquo;pre-emptive correction&rsquo; (nit-picking) and instead look for ways in which to bring encouragement and words of affirmation to my children.</p>
<p>The Lord is so merciful and kind to us!  I am so grateful that He chose to gently remind me of my need for change (repentance) in how I speak to my children.</p>
