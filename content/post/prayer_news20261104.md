### The Great Commission and Jesus' New Commandment
##### (This is a short version of a message I often give at churches.) 

_And Jesus came and said to them, “All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you. And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age.”_ ~ Matthew 28:18-20 | ESV

There are several verbs which we can see in Matthew’s version of the Great Commission: _go, make, baptize, teach_. Briefly, I want to look at the phrase that contains the verb _teach_:

Look at _"...teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you,"_ and consider what it means. In John's gospel chapter 13, set during what we call 'the Last Supper,' Jesus does a few surprising things. Firstly, he washes the disciples' feet (John 13:3-11). You may recall from Luke's account that the disciples were vying for position, arguing among themselves as to who was the greatest (Luke 22:24). Secondly, He says that one of the twelve is a traitor and subsequently dismisses Judas Iscariot. Thirdly - and this is what I want to focus on - He gives a new commandment:

 _"A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another. By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for **one another**."_ ~ John 13:34-35 | ESV
 
Amidst all of the unhappiness and confusion going on in the hearts of the disciples in that room - a dimly lit one, I imagine - Jesus gives the remaining eleven men an impossible command to, _"love one another: just as I have loved you...."_

We know historically that the disciples, through the power of Holy Spirit, did just that. They each went on to practice loving one another, teaching others to do the same. In fact, the majority of the episitles contain the phrase "one another" multiple times, aimed specifically at believers and always practical, whether an exhortation to right action or a prohibition. Each one of the _one anothers_ should be looked at as the practical outworking of what loving one another looks like.

Here are some examples of the approximately sixty _one anothers_ (in the ESV):

* _Live in harmony with **one another**. Do not be haughty, but associate with the lowly. Never be wise in your own sight._ ~ <small>Romans 12:16</small>
* _For you were called to freedom, brothers. Only do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh, but through love serve **one another**_. ~ <small>Galatians 5:13</small>
* _Bear **one another’s** burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ._ ~ <small>Galatians 6:2</small>	
* _Be kind to **one another**, tender hearted, forgiving **one another**, as God in Christ forgave you._ ~ <small>Ephesians 4:32</small> 
* _Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count **others** more significant than yourselves._ ~ <small>Philippians 2:3</small>
* _Do not lie to **one another**, seeing that you have put off the old self with its practices...._ ~ <small>Colossians 3:9</small> 
* _Therefore encourage **one another** and build **one another** up, just as you are doing._ ~ <small>I Thessalonians 5:11</small>
* _See that no one repays anyone evil for evil, but always seek to do good to **one another** and to everyone._ ~ <small>I Thessalonians 5:15</small>	
* _And let us consider how to stir up **one another** to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together, as is the habit of some, but encouraging **one another**, and all the more as you see the Day drawing near._ ~ <small>Hebrews 10:24-25</small>  
* _Therefore, confess your sins to **one another** and pray for one another, that you may be healed. The prayer of a righteous person has great power as it is working._ ~ <small>James 5:16</small>
* _Above all, keep loving **one another** earnestly, since love covers a multitude of sins. Show hospitality to **one another** without grumbling. As each has received a gift, use it to serve **one another**, as good stewards of God’s varied grace...._ ~ <small>I Peter 4:8-10</small>
* _And this is his commandment, that we believe in the name of his Son Jesus Christ and love **one another**, just as he has commanded us._ ~ <small>I John 3:23</small>

Every time I read this list or see the a _one another_ verse in the Bible, I need to be asking myself, "Am I intentionally seeking to practice this?" As a disciple of Christ, I am not only empowered by God to practice loving my fellow brothers and sisters (i.e. the church) but I must also realize that I am free to do so, no longer enslaved to sin. I _get_ to practice the one anothers.
